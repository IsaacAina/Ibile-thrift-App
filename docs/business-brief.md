# Business Brief

## The business

**Ibile Thrift & Credit Cooperative Society Ltd**, Mushin, Lagos. Founded 2011, about 1,400 members: market traders, tailors, artisans and bus operators. Members pay weekly or monthly contributions, hold savings, take loans against those savings, and repay in instalments. Today everything lives in an exercise-book ledger and one treasurer's Excel file. The product is a member-facing app and WhatsApp-style assistant that sits on top of that ledger.

## The person who uses it

Mrs. Folake Adeyemi, 34 to 58, runs a provisions stall. She uses a mid-range Android phone (Tecno or Infinix, 3 GB RAM, cracked screen is normal). Connection is 3G, often 2G, dropping in and out; data bundles are bought in small amounts, so every megabyte costs her. She is comfortable with WhatsApp, bank transfer apps and airtime top-ups, but not with spreadsheets, menus within menus, or financial jargon. She is often using it standing in a shop, with one hand, in daylight glare.

## The one thing that must work

She opens the app and sees her **true, current position** (savings, loan outstanding, last contribution, next due amount) in under five seconds on a weak connection, and it matches the ledger exactly. If the last-known figure is cached, it says so with a timestamp. A wrong or ambiguous balance destroys trust in the society itself.

## Three questions it gets asked repeatedly that only it can answer

1. **"How much can I borrow today, and when would I get it?"** The answer depends on her own savings, months of unbroken contributions, existing loan, and guarantor availability under this society's bye-laws.
2. **"Why is my loan balance ₦212,400 when I have paid for eight months?"** Only this system holds her repayment schedule, interest method and every posted payment.
3. **"If I withdraw or leave now, what do I actually get back?"** This nets savings against outstanding loans, notice periods and charges, using her figures and the society's rules.

## Why the interface must feel a particular way

It must feel like a **trusted passbook**: calm, plain, official and unhurried. Members are handling money that took months of small sacrifices to build, and many have been burned by loan apps and informal schemes. So:

- Numbers first, in large type, in naira, with no decoration around them.
- Everyday words ("You can borrow up to ₦180,000"), never "eligibility threshold".
- Every figure shows where it came from and when it was last updated.
- Light, fast screens that work offline from the last sync and never make her wait on a spinner.
- No dark patterns, no upsells, no urgency. A confirmation receipt after every action.

If it feels like a fintech gimmick, she will go back to asking the treasurer.
